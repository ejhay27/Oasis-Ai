# Targeted research landscape review

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-12. This is a short desk review to inform an initial feasibility ADR, not a systematic literature review. It does not audit code/data/model licenses, reproduce results, or recommend downloading any third-party weights/dataset.

## Representative architecture and training patterns

1. **Spacetime latent patches and large-scale DiTs.** OpenAI's Sora technical report describes a video-compression network, spatiotemporal latent patches, and a transformer denoiser operating on compressed video representations; it also discusses training on large quantities of videos with text captions. This demonstrates a scalable representation but does not establish a feasible local training plan or grant access to its data/weights. [OpenAI, “Video generation models as world simulators” (2024)](https://openai.com/index/video-generation-models-as-world-simulators/).
2. **Causal compression and factorized window attention.** W.A.L.T. uses a shared causal image/video latent space, spatial and spatiotemporal window-attention layers, and text cross-attention; its authors report benchmarks on UCF-101, Kinetics-600, and ImageNet. This is evidence that latent compression and attention factorization are important architecture levers, not that OASIS should copy its code or weights. [Gupta et al., W.A.L.T. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06662).
3. **Joint video-VAE/denoiser design.** LTX-Video describes a transformer-based latent diffusion system with a highly compressed 3D video VAE and a decoder participating in final denoising; its paper reports text-to-video and image-to-video capabilities. That direction can reduce token/activation cost, but requires a video autoencoder and its training/reconstruction evaluation. If any pretrained component is reused, creator/license/inherited weights must be tracked. [HaCohen et al., LTX-Video (2024 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00103).
4. **Progressive training and weight provenance.** The Open-Sora 2.0 report describes low-resolution text-to-video, low-resolution text/image-to-video, then high-resolution fine-tuning. It reports a total training estimate of roughly $199.6K assuming $2 per H200 GPU-hour, and it says the DiT initializes from an 11B-parameter Flux image model; the report separately describes a video autoencoder trained from scratch. Those are the authors' reported setup and costs, not a transferable OASIS budget or price quote. This is a useful example of why a trained component and a fine-tuned/inherited model need separate provenance. Its public code's license does not itself clear the training data or all weights for OASIS use. [Open-Sora 2.0 report, arXiv:2503.09642 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09642).
5. **Evaluation beyond a single score.** A September 2026 survey reviews post-training/alignment and groups evaluation by alignment dimensions; it discusses text-video metrics, multi-dimensional suites such as VBench/VBench2, and human evaluation, while noting long-horizon consistency and safety challenges. It reinforces the decision not to treat a single loss or metric as video quality. [Li et al., “Video Generation Models: A Survey of Post-Training and Alignment” (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00812).

## Implications for OASIS

- The long-term field uses latent video compression, spatiotemporal transformers, richer pretrained text encoders, large curated video-text data, staged training, and significant accelerator budgets. Our 8×16×16 pixel-space model is intentionally a **pipeline feasibility experiment**, not a competitive architecture.
- The tiny code-first model avoids inherited weights for the initial provenance exercise. It sacrifices visual detail, language understanding, temporal reach, and efficiency. Results from it must not be generalized to commercial video generation.
- Text-to-video and image-to-video are separately trained/evaluated capabilities. This prototype implements only the former.
- A later latent model may be more practical, but only after selecting a lawful, documented autoencoder/encoder strategy and measuring a real small run. Adopting open code or public weights requires component-by-component licensing and provenance review.
- Benchmarking needs controlled prompts, a stable held-out test set, explicit preprocessing/seeds/sample counts, multi-dimensional metrics where valid, and human review. External comparisons require rights-compatible models and fair conditions.
- Video captions and web-scale datasets are a quality and rights risk. This repository does not select or approve a public dataset based on availability alone.

## Open work

A systematic literature/benchmark review, source-by-source dataset-rights assessment, licensing audit of candidate external models/components, and measured compute/cost study remain open. No third-party code, weights, or data from the sources above have been downloaded or integrated by this research-infrastructure increment.
